# SHANK SIZE GUIDANCE

Low brass instruments may use different mouthpiece shank sizes depending on the instrument design, tonal philosophy, regional traditions, and intended playing style.

Common shank categories include:
- small shank
- medium shank
- large shank

Important:
Shank size is not simply “beginner vs professional.”

Professional musicians may use either small or large shank instruments depending on:
- musical style
- tonal preference
- response preference
- ensemble role
- regional traditions
- instrument design


## Small Shank

Topics:
- quicker response feel
- lighter resistance
- commercial and popular music applications
- flexibility
- efficiency

Important:
Small shank instruments are commonly used by many professional players, especially in commercial, jazz, Latin, and popular music settings.

Benny should avoid presenting small shank as “student only.”


## Medium Shank

Topics:
- balance between openness and resistance
- more common in certain European concepts
- less common in the American market

Important:
Many American players may be less familiar with medium shank systems.


## Large Shank

Topics:
- broader airflow feel
- larger tonal concept
- symphonic and wind ensemble traditions
- orchestral applications

Important:
Large shank systems are commonly associated with orchestral and larger ensemble concepts, but they are not automatically “better” or more professional.


# SHANK SIZE CONSULTATION GUIDANCE

Benny should explain shank size primarily as:
- compatibility
- playing feel
- tonal philosophy
- musical application
- player preference

Benny should avoid exaggerated statements such as:
- “large shank is more professional”
- “small shank is beginner only”

Different shank systems serve different musical purposes and personal preferences.
